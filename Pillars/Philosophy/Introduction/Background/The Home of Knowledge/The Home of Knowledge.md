# The Home of Knowledge

Knowledge needs a place to live - somewhere with structure, boundaries, and stability. Without a home, the cycle of knowledge produces activity without memory.

That home is a **Knowledge Island**: a discrete, self-contained body of knowledge with defined boundaries and internal geography.

At the heart of every island is **the Capital** - the settled civic centre where canonical, ratified knowledge is held. The Capital contains two buildings:

**The Library** is the canonical record. It holds two zones - Pillars (internal knowledge owned by the island: methodology, approach, domain reference) and Resources (external reference material that exists independently of the island). The Library is version-controlled and is the single source of truth. Nothing in it changes except through the governance process.

**The Council Hall** is the civic governance space. Not a store - it is where proposals are reviewed, decisions are made, and the island's direction is set.

Beside the Library lies **the Calendar** - time-bound notes: daily, meeting, session and periodic reviews. It is not part of the Library; its entries are dated records kept as they happen, and need no Enactment record.

Beyond the Capital, **Streams** are the island's lifeblood - flowing water that irrigates the landscape and sustains the agents who live and work within it. They carry knowledge in motion: active projects, evolving ideas, ongoing work. Streams are not part of the Library; their content is not canonical. The lifecycle is: emerge as a Stream → mature through work → stabilise into Pillars or Resources → the Stream is retired.

At the shore lies **the Port** - the island's point of entry and exit. Material arrives by dock, rail depot or airport, passes the customs house, and is assessed before being routed inward; what leaves the island goes out the same way. Nothing flows directly from the Port into the Library; it is assessed first, relevant material routed to the right Stream or zone, the rest discarded.
