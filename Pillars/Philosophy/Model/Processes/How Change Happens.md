# How Change Happens

## Overview

The Processes chapter holds the operational mechanisms through which governance is enacted in practice. These are not policies or conventions - they are the active procedures that move knowledge from idea to ratified content, and that govern how the Knowledge Islands model itself propagates across the ecosystem.

Four processes are documented here; this note introduces the two that govern change. The [Acquisition Process](<Acquisition Process/Acquisition Process.md>) and the [Work Process](<Work Process/Work Process.md>) describe how external material arrives and how coordinated work moves. The **Enactment Process** is internal: it governs how any island moves a proposal through its own council to ratification. The **Contribution Process** is external: it governs how knowledge developed in one territory can be proposed as a canonical addition to the shared Knowledge Islands model. Both processes work in concert with [Jurisdiction](../../Introduction/Concept/Jurisdiction/Jurisdiction.md), which defines who counts as a Citizen, what council membership means, and what authority the council holds.

---

## Enactment Process

The Enactment Process is the internal change mechanism for a Knowledge Island. It describes the stages a proposal passes through - from draft to council review to ratification - and the responsibilities of each participant at each stage. Any change to island content that requires formal approval passes through this process.

## Contribution Process

The Contribution Process is the external propagation mechanism: the procedure by which knowledge developed in one island or territory can be proposed as a canonical addition to the shared Knowledge Islands model. It is the route through which the portable model grows, and the check that prevents island-specific content from contaminating the shared standard.
