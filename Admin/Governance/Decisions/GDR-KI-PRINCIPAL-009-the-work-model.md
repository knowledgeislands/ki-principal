# GDR-KI-PRINCIPAL-009: The work model

## Context

Kris works with agents through coordinating sessions that brief background runs, collect their reports and put decisions to Kris. That work was carried by three separate instruments: a thread, named after the chat session; a checkpoint holding its pick-up state; and a thread folder holding its decisions log and reports. The same work could be described by any of them, "hold" carried four meanings, and nothing said where a piece of work lay or for how long. The ways of working Project designed one model of work to replace them, and Kris answered its queries on 18 October 2026.

Kris set the unit: "the unit is work, not a conversation". Kris named the two forms of work: "regular work and project work. That's the two forms of work that happen, right?", and the states: "It's either with someone or it's been handed off to someone or we've agreed effectively that it's like on hold ... or we can say it's done, at which point that area is concluded." Kris placed the logbook: "The logbook could be the [Git] history. Yeah, I think it's probably the Git history." On regular work, Kris answered: "there's no coordination thread needed for it because it is sort of self-coordinating, but there's no reason why the state of play thread can't sort of talk about it". On themes: "it makes sense for themes themselves to have a page because you can document why it's a theme, what's important about it, any general direction, and guidance", and on Project notes, "I think they should be flat in streams/projects". On the words, Kris agreed them while noting "We probably just need to see how it comes out in use". Later that day Kris settled the banner glyphs, "with worker, I think you just need a robot saying with a robot", and the answers, "the new reply block is better", having said "I'm starting to outlive the copy block ... Maybe the best approach is just for me to talk like this if it's clear."

A single work file holding state, next steps, messages and parked query tags grows into a catch-all, and nothing in it says what the work is for, so new work cannot be routed by reading it. On 19 October 2026 Kris asked that "work should have a package idea and it should be clear about what its goals are and maybe a description to help things route it, so we've got that kind of progressive reveal capability", named it the work package, and settled routing: "once it's given it, it has to do something with it, it can't reject it, it can then say it wants it to be given to someone else", and "everything should be trying to continually improve and I think that should be coordinated by state of play". On priority Kris said: "if something's added to the top of it with an important flag, it must be processed in that order and that allows the state of play to tell something to change its way of working".

## Decision

Knowledge Islands organises coordinated work under one work model. The Work Process note in this island's Pillars describes it in full; this record states what is decided.

- **The unit.** Work is the unit. A piece of work is a work package: a definition of what it is, its goals and what it takes, read first, and its state, queries, incoming messages and own decisions, read when needed. A chat session is only the channel through which a party works on it, and the work outlives every session.
- **Levels and forms.** Work lies at three levels and cascades down to the lowest level that can do it; coordination sits above execution and attention rolls back up.
  - **Territory work**, id `ki` in this territory, is the state of play: it coordinates the coordinators, routes new work and stewards the method. It may coordinate explicitly assigned Project packages; execution remains with work items and their runs or people.
  - **Regular work** and **Project work** are the two forms of work, side by side beneath it. Regular work, id `ki.regular`, is the territory's recurring work, defined at territory level in the Capital's Activity notes. It is self-coordinating: it needs no coordination thread, never closes, and is not necessarily dispatched by state of play, which may still discuss it. Ad hoc work with no Project lies in it as `ki.regular.<slug>`. Project work, id `ki.<project>`, coordinates a Project and is finite. A Project's own recurring checks stay with the Project and end with it. These ids are current examples, retained alongside the approved typed target below.
  - **A work item** is one record in any repository, created by either form and executed by a run or a person. Its record's status is its state.
- **Themes.** A theme is a label that regular work, Project work or ad hoc work can carry, not a level. Each theme has one page in `Streams/Themes/` saying why it is a theme, what matters about it, its general direction and the guidance common to the work under it, and it holds no work. Project notes stay flat in `Streams/Projects/` with a `theme:` key. No identifier carries a theme.
- **Roles.** Every handoff has a worker and a supervisor, relative to that handoff: a coordinating agent is worker to Kris and supervisor to its runs. A stuck worker sends the work back to its supervisor with a blocking query. The holder, the party the work lies with now, is derived from the state and the two roles.
- **States.** Work is in one of seven states: With worker; With supervisor; Handoff to supervisor; Handoff to worker; Handoff to a named target; On hold; Finished. Only the supervisor's receive and the hold are set; every other state is derived from where the work's items lie. Only the holder moves the work, by sending it into handoff or receiving it out of handoff.
- **On hold.** On hold is agreed by the supervisor from With supervisor, with one of two reasons: paused, with a named release condition, or more expected, released when new work arrives. The worker drains on the way in, and the move is a mark: every touched repository is committed and pushed, each open query is parked in the queries file, and the state file records the reason, release and since. Resume or new work moves it to Handoff to worker; finish moves it to Finished. On hold has no heartbeat and is never stuck; a paused hold older than 31 days is flagged.
- **The roadmap mapping.** The states are the roadmap's machine one level up. `in-progress` is With worker, `awaiting-review` Handoff to supervisor, `triage` and an unapproved `draft` With supervisor, `ready` Handoff to worker, a blocker or a `waiting-for` hold a handoff to its target, a `parked` hold On hold paused, and `done` or `cancelled` Finished. Work items need no new fields.
- **Stuck and the heartbeat.** A heartbeat runs only while work lies with a worker: an update on change, at least every 5 minutes and always by 10. A worker's item that has neither moved nor beaten for 10 minutes is stuck. Work with the supervisor is never stuck, only older.
- **Words.** Hold means only the agreed On hold state; drain is the activity on the way into it. The supervisor receives with `with me`, `ack`, `got it` or `seen`, agrees a hold with `pause` or `done for now`, and moves the work with `resume` and `finish`. The views by holder are with you, stuck, blocked, moving and on hold. `+` and `-` are working areas.
- **Homes.** Each piece of work above a work item is a work package in one flat folder in the working area, `+/_WORK/<id>/`, the work folder. A package has exactly five files, all created with it: `work.md`, the definition, with a routing description, goals and scope; `state.md`, the state fields, now, next steps and held work; `queries.md`, every open and parked query in full; `messages.md`, the inbox; and `decisions.md`. Run reports, open briefs and design papers sit beside them, and nothing in the package is a log. `Streams/` holds only definitions and records: Project notes, theme pages and work items. Links point upward, from the work file to its Project note. The run state folder holds only runtime state.
- **The inbox.** A work's incoming messages are a queue taken in priority order; the worker may group similar items, and a line added at the top marked important is taken first, in that order. Its assigned steward acts as the package's own coordinator. Other work, including an unassigned State of play, writes only a line to its messages, or replaces its own line not yet acted on. Persist each owner answer before dispatching work it authorises, honour dependencies and existing authority, then continue unaffected requests while background work proceeds; report and approval policy stay as they are.
- **Routing.** State of play is the single dispatch coordinator: each new unit of work without a clear owner, and each method improvement, comes to it, and it routes the unit to one work by matching the works' descriptions. Already-owned units go direct. A routed work cannot reject the unit: it does what it can with it, raises a query for what needs Kris, and gives back to State of play only the part it judges is not its own, with a reason. A unit is returned once and then goes to Kris. Routing confers no authority to execute.
- **Stewardship.** Every work tries to improve continually and State of play coordinates it, dispatching each improvement as small regular work or into the Project it most affects; a significant change revives the Ways of working Project, or a more targeted Project under the Model theme. Each state file records the method revision its worker last read, and a change of method is sent as a targeted refresh line only to the works it affects that are behind.
- **History.** The Git history of a work folder is its logbook. Each session commits each completed, verified unit by explicit path before its turn ends. Move subjects name the affected package and, where useful, the acting work; a coherent move stays one commit. Fast-forward pulls run only into a clean checkout. Publication follows the standing push rule, with inspection of every unpublished ancestor and its risk and ref limits; cadence and working-area paths grant no extra publication authority. No messages merge driver or append-only conflict policy is adopted. Shared history is never rewritten: the working area is pruned at every mark, and a separate working repository is considered only if size measurably hurts.
- **Decisions.** `decisions.md` stays apart from the logbook and holds Kris's decisions about that work, in Kris's words, in the turn they arrive. Relays, sends and operational events are moves, recorded as commits, not decisions. At each mark each entry is checked against its durable home and then struck, reduced to a one-line pointer, or kept; entries carry no running numbers.
- **The banner.** A report shows its state in rich text: a heading whose glyphs end with whose move it is, the state in role words, a line of counts and a list of items with holder and since. A settled state shows its holder's glyph alone, as in `🤖 With worker`; an arrow appears only in a handoff state, as in `🤖 → 👤 Handoff to supervisor` or `→ 🎯 Handoff to <target>`. The report's form line carries the same prefix, and the send line appears only when the work is in handoff to Kris.
- **Answers.** An owner report carries no copyable reply block. Queries keep their Context, Query and Options with confidence; Kris answers in speech, and the worker reads each answer back as taken in the decisions log and asks one line where an answer is unclear.
- **The observatory.** Work state is read through one `ki` command, `ki work status --format json`, schema `ki/work-status/v1`, one entry per work package, from which the five views are derived.
- **Assignment and handover.** Each Project package has one explicitly assigned coordinating work, either its own Project coordination or a State of play, distinct from its holder. Assignment neither receives work nor releases an On hold state. Opening or closing a dedicated thread requests handover; the old steward reconciles runs in flight, queued launches and pending writes, stops launching into the package and commits the change in one coherent write. The new steward acknowledges before acting and takes supervision of the reconciled runs. A missing binding, closed or quiet session, old heartbeat or timeout transfers nothing. STEWARD-FIELD remains undecided: no field, value or file encoding is chosen; assignment is logged in the stewarding work's decisions under the harness standard until that decision.
- **Packages and threads.** A session is a runtime channel; its thread is seated in one package and may act in others assigned to its coordinating work. Package and thread are not one-to-one. The assigned steward is the package's coordinator for every entry-point purpose, including tidying, and may brief runs into it; others send messages. Each brief names the reporting package separately from the product repository, and reports go to that package whoever launched them. One turn acts in one package. Opening or assigning a Project package requires a registered Project within the cap, first-message seeding and the state supported by the actual move, never an invented With worker. The dormant first-message On hold exception remains; a Project need not immediately have a package or thread. The cap counts packages neither On hold nor Finished.
- **Per-package attention.** One territory Summary names the work on each item and qualifies queries by work id and tag. Each package retains its own state, since, mark, beat, queries and decisions. A territory mark never moves Project marks; acknowledgement moves only named work. A cross-Project decision is kept once at its territory home. A territory hold drains assigned packages and asks separately stewarded packages to drain rather than writing their state.
- **Relationships.** Every package below the main State of play names its coordinating State of play as `parent`, including secondary, Project, regular and ad hoc packages; an ad hoc parent is not inferred from its regular-work prefix. The main keeps `covers` as its checked register, including coverage through secondaries, before package creation and after pruning. Secondary lists remain assigned subsets. Relations agree within one territory, with unique parentage, unique secondary coverage and no cycles; coverage is not inferred from package presence. Threads carry no committed metadata.
- **Record and Project identity.** A record's `project` resolves through the registry to its Project, territory and package, but opens no package, grants no execution authority and moves no record from its owning repository. Holds, readiness, priority and acceptance stay there; the assigned steward updates next steps. Project-less records go first to an existing regular or ad hoc owner; only unresolved ownership goes to State of play. Reclassification reconciles runs and reporting homes. One record may motivate a finite-outcome Project: register a separate Project note, link the original by its `project` slug and retain its id, history and status without duplicate delivery. Registration alone creates no package or thread.
- **Principal home and discovery.** Capital/principal is the default home for Project, regular and ad hoc packages. A member-repository home is a deliberate exception declared in `work.md` Scope, not a transfer of execution, record, priority or acceptance ownership. This narrows the former regular/ad hoc placement exception; the corresponding harness contract must be aligned before consumers or writers rely on it, with no relocation grant inferred. A principal-seated thread identifies its Capital, reads Charter and governed inventory, resolves exact registered checkouts, then reads each execution repository's rules. Missing or ambiguous bindings await receiver resolution; no root is guessed. A repository binding does not prove a live thread's working directory.
- **Host requirement and seat.** A thread's seat is never committed in a package, hold or report. A host or category requirement is a work fact at the smallest applicable message, brief, record or package scope, resolved through territory machine inventory at bootstrap and re-anchoring. It restricts device-dependent execution only, leaving acknowledgement, planning, queries and dispatch possible in queue order. Work without an eligible host stays visible and age-flagged. Concrete-host and category meanings must be settled before syntax adoption; no host syntax or field is introduced here.
- **Typed identity and coexistence.** The approved package-id target is `<territory>.<type>.<name>`; type distinguishes State of play, Project and regular work, with ad hoc work using the regular form. Themes and hosts are absent; parentage is declared. Current ids, paths and references remain valid. Readers must accept current and typed identities before writers emit typed identities or anyone creates or renames a package using them. Historical old-id readability and lookup have no time limit; resolution identifies one package without duplicates or ambiguity. Receiving owners settle stored spelling, umbrella suffixes and compatibility mechanism through explicit delivery decisions and collision checks. A record id, Project registry slug and package id remain distinct: `project` keeps its registry slug, never a dotted package id. Runtime slugs are not reverse identity mappings. The pattern implies no reverse-domain repository rename or physical root.

## Consequences

- The cascade changes coordination and reporting, not the seven states, roles, banner, report policy or roadmap mapping. Pilot exit conditions remain with their rollout work; this amendment neither performs nor accepts a pilot. AT-REST implementation, STEWARD-FIELD, account steering and host syntax remain outside this amendment.
- The Work Process note is the one canonical description of the model. The harness standards and skills that govern coordination, delegation, the work package, forward work, Activities and recurring work cite it and do not restate it; `ki-work-package` owns the package's exact form.
- Existing single-file and package readers retain their compatibility. Typed writers and migration wait for compatible identity and placement readers in the harness and tools-ki; this amendment delivers neither consumer support nor a package creation or rename instruction.
- Migration is separately authorised after reader readiness, receiving-steward acknowledgement, an exact current incoming-reference and mutable-binding manifest, bounded mutation authority, writer/queue quiescence and verified rollback. Immutable native-session identity, original reports and message provenance, ownership, holds and marks are preserved. A rename neither receives work nor releases a hold; existing instruments govern until their replacements support the model. No registry rebinding or migration is performed by this decision.
- These earlier choices no longer govern: checkpoints and thread folders as separate instruments, and the pick-up block; briefs held in the run state folder; three-part thread ids carrying the Initiative and the former thread-specific `.sop` identity (distinct from a type in the approved package-id target); a decisions log beside the Project note in `Streams/`; nesting Project notes under their Initiative; Initiatives as the home of recurring work; state of play dispatching every occurrence of regular work; design papers kept beside a Project or Initiative in `Streams/`; the block-letter footer frames and the resume line; the copyable stacked reply block; and `hold` as a receive word.
- Initiatives become themes, and records, Activity notes and Project notes name an optional `theme` where they named an `initiative`.
- An Observatory work instrument reads the `ki work status` contract once `tools-ki` provides it.
